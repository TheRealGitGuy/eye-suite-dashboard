The rules

Every row and every column holds the digits 1–6 exactly once. The grid is divided into cages (heavy outlines). The small clue in a cage's corner is a target and an operation: the digits in that cage must combine to the target. For − and ÷ (always two-cell cages) take the larger digit first. A one-cell cage simply tells you its digit. Digits may repeat inside a cage as long as they are not in the same row or column.

How each puzzle is built

  1. A seed, not a file. Every puzzle is named by its level and number, e.g. Hard #0042. That name is hashed into a seed for a small random-number generator (mulberry32), so the same name always rebuilds exactly the same puzzle on any device. Nothing is downloaded, yet there are 5,000 puzzles per level — 20,000 in total.
  2. The answer comes first. A random 6×6 Latin square (every row and column 1–6) is built by randomised backtracking. This is the hidden solution.
  3. Cages are grown. Starting from random unclaimed cells, cages "grow" into neighbouring cells until they reach a size drawn from the level's size profile. Easy uses mostly 2-cell cages and up to four free single-cell clues; Super hard uses 2- to 5-cell cages and no free clues.
  4. Operations are chosen. Two-cell cages get + − × ÷ (÷ only when it divides exactly); larger cages get + or ×. The target is computed from the hidden solution.
  5. A logic solver grades it. The puzzle is handed to a solver that only uses human-style techniques, always trying the simplest first and recording the hardest one it needed (table below).
  6. Keep or throw away. If the grade matches the level being built, the puzzle is kept; otherwise the next seed (attempt 2, 3, …) is tried. A puzzle the solver can finish by pure logic is proved to have exactly one answer. Super-hard candidates are additionally checked by a brute-force counter that must find exactly one solution. Easy puzzles typically take ~9 tries, Super hard ~30.

What makes a level

LevelHardest technique needed
EasySingles only: a cell with one possible digit, a digit with one possible home in a row/column, and the options for 1- and 2-cell cages.
MildCombination work on 3+ cell cages: listing which sets of digits can make e.g. 13+ across three cells and ruling out the rest.
HardInteractions: a cage that must put a digit in part of a row removes it from the rest of that row; naked pairs/triples and hidden pairs.
Super hardAll of the above get stuck. You must reason "if this cell were 4… that breaks something", i.e. trial and contradiction, at least once.

How hints work

Hint acts like a tutor. It looks at your grid and your notes, finds the simplest logical next step and explains it. First you get a nudge (e.g. "Look at column 4. Where can the 5 go?") with the relevant squares outlined. Tap Explain or Hint again for the full reasoning, and Apply to make the move for you. It checks first for mistakes: a wrong answer, or notes that have ruled out the real digit. On the hardest puzzles it uses a "what if" test, showing step by step why an option breaks the puzzle. Reveal a square is still there as a last resort. Any hint stops that solve from counting as a best time.

What is saved

Your progress is stored in cookies on this device: puzzles started and solved per level, best / average / last times, hints, checks and mistakes, a daily solving streak, which puzzle numbers you've solved, and your unfinished grid on each level (so you can switch levels and come back). If this browser blocks cookies, the app falls back to local browser storage, and says so on the Stats page.
